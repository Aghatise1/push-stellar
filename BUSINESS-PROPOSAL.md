# Push business proposal

## Executive summary

Push is building a work-coordination and settlement-record platform for independent workers and the clients who hire them. It keeps the agreed brief, private communication, delivery evidence, approval and payment reference in one connected record.

The current product is an MVP running through a controlled, invite-only Stellar testnet pilot. The invitation restriction applies to this testing phase, not to the intended public mainnet product. The MVP demonstrates the full workflow without custodying user funds or issuing a proprietary token. The next objective is a controlled pilot that proves people can complete real work with less ambiguity and stronger payment evidence.

## The problem

Independent work is often coordinated across job boards, direct messages, email, cloud folders and separate payment tools. This creates recurring failures:

- the scope changes without a reliable record;
- work is delivered without clear acceptance criteria;
- decisions and revision requests become difficult to prove;
- payment evidence is separated from the work it settled;
- a worker's useful history is trapped across unrelated platforms.

For Web3 teams, stablecoin settlement can move quickly while the agreement and delivery evidence remain fragmented.

## The solution

Push creates one accountable work flow:

1. A client records the outcome, deliverables, budget and deadline.
2. Workers apply against those visible terms.
3. The selected worker accepts a preserved copy of the agreement.
4. Messages, delivery evidence and revision decisions stay with the assignment.
5. Approval records the selected payment route and its verification reference.

Push currently verifies direct Stellar testnet USDC settlement without collecting private keys. Bank/card and other payment routes remain simulations until compliant providers and operating countries are selected.

## Initial customer

The first market should be narrow:

- small Web3 teams, community projects and early-stage founders hiring short, clearly scoped work;
- independent designers, developers, editors and community contributors who need stronger proof of agreement, delivery and payment;
- transactions that can be completed and reviewed within days or weeks.

Push should avoid trying to replace every freelance marketplace during the pilot. The first promise is a better work record for a small number of real assignments.

## Why Stellar fits

Stellar is useful to Push where it improves settlement:

- low-cost, fast transfers;
- stablecoin support;
- public transaction evidence;
- a non-custodial route in which users control their wallets;
- an ecosystem of teams already coordinating cross-border digital work.

Blockchain is infrastructure in this proposal. Push succeeds only if clients and workers find the complete workflow useful, even when they do not care about the chain underneath it.

## Current proof

The working MVP includes:

- accounts and email verification;
- private-by-default profiles;
- job briefs, applications and worker selection;
- preserved assignment terms;
- participant-only messaging;
- delivery, revision, approval and dispute states;
- wallet address connection and payment requests;
- server-side Stellar testnet payment verification;
- an event history connecting work and settlement evidence.

This is product proof, not market traction. User, revenue and payment-volume claims should be made only after measured pilot activity exists.

## Business model hypothesis

The pilot should test a transparent completion fee charged only when an assignment is successfully settled. A reasonable research range is **5% to 10%**, with the payer and exact rate selected after interviews and pilot observation.

Possible later revenue lines include:

- team subscriptions for reporting, permissions and repeat hiring;
- optional verification or dispute services;
- compliant payment-provider revenue share where permitted;
- ecosystem implementation work for organisations that need accountable contributor payments.

Push should not introduce a token as a revenue model.

## Go-to-market plan

1. Recruit 10–20 vetted testers from the Stellar West Africa and wider builder communities.
2. Run 5–10 tightly scoped test assignments from brief through payment evidence.
3. Interview both sides immediately after each completed or abandoned assignment.
4. Publish anonymised, permissioned case studies showing what became clearer or faster.
5. Use verified pilot results in SCF, Instawards, hackathon and investor applications.

The first growth loop is completed work: a client brings a job, the worker completes it, both leave with a useful record, and either participant brings the next assignment.

## Metrics that matter

- briefs that receive at least one qualified application;
- selected workers who accept the preserved terms;
- assignments reaching delivery;
- assignments reaching verified settlement;
- median time from brief to worker selection;
- median time from delivery to approval;
- revision and dispute rates;
- repeat use by clients and workers;
- reasons people abandon a workflow stage.

Follower counts and testnet transaction volume are supporting signals, not primary evidence of product value.

## Proposed 30-day funded sprint

**Objective:** prove that invited clients and workers can complete a real assignment through Push with a clear work and payment record.

**Week 1 — Reliability**

- close critical workflow and email defects;
- verify mobile and desktop paths;
- finalise pilot rules, privacy boundaries and support process.

**Week 2 — Pilot onboarding**

- onboard vetted clients and workers;
- publish concise tester instructions;
- observe the first briefs, applications and accepted assignments.

**Week 3 — Completion and evidence**

- support delivery, revisions and Stellar testnet settlement verification;
- collect structured feedback and failure reasons;
- fix defects that block completion.

**Week 4 — Results**

- publish a measured sprint report;
- document completed workflows and lessons;
- define the next production, compliance and security gates.

## Funding use

A small grant should fund a specific sprint rather than an undefined launch:

- product engineering and testing;
- managed hosting, monitoring and email delivery;
- independent security review preparation;
- user research and pilot support;
- design, documentation and demonstration material;
- legal and payment-provider discovery for the initial operating market.

Any budget should state exact amounts, owners and deliverables before submission.

## Material risks

- **Trust and disputes:** the current product records disputes but does not yet provide production-grade adjudication.
- **Payments and regulation:** production stablecoin or bank/card flows require country, provider and compliance decisions.
- **Marketplace liquidity:** clients and workers must arrive in sufficient concentration for jobs to match.
- **Security:** production use requires independent review, monitoring, backups and incident response.
- **Positioning:** the name and “earn” domain can be mistaken for a task-reward scheme unless every public surface leads with real work and clear records.

These risks are reasons to run a controlled pilot with measurable gates, not reasons to pretend the product is already finished.

## Grant-ready description

> Push is a work-coordination and settlement-record platform for Web3 teams and independent workers. It preserves the agreed brief, private delivery process, decisions and payment evidence in one accountable flow. The current invite-only pilot uses Stellar testnet to demonstrate non-custodial USDC settlement verification safely; the intended mainnet product is designed for public access with human profile review. In a focused 30-day sprint, we will onboard vetted testers, complete real end-to-end work flows, measure where participants succeed or abandon the process, fix blocking defects and publish an evidence-based pilot report for the Stellar ecosystem.

## Decision gates before mainnet

Push should move beyond testnet only after it has:

1. evidence from completed pilot assignments;
2. approved fee, refund and dispute policies;
3. a defined legal operator and initial country scope;
4. production payment and wallet-ownership controls;
5. an independent security review;
6. backups, monitoring and incident response;
7. clear support and moderation ownership.
